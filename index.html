Swedish Labour Market Intelligence

Recruitment demand Arbetsförmedlingen

Question: Where are Swedish employers hiring, for which occupations, and what do they ask for? 500 job ads and 327 requirement records go through an SSIS pipeline into a SQL Server star schema, then into Power BI.
Findings: Stockholm holds 28.6% of all ads, staffing agencies dominate the top employers, and Swedish is the most-requested requirement: 72% of ads that name a language ask for it.

Swedish Staffing Profitability

Bemanning Company financials

Question: Which Swedish staffing companies are actually profitable, and how has the industry held up as hiring demand cooled? Five years of annual accounts (2021–2025) for 23 staffing companies, set against SCB job-vacancy statistics.
Findings: Combined revenue fell 28% from its 2022 peak to 15.1 bn SEK, the operating margin has been negative since 2023, and 13 of 23 companies made an operating loss in 2025. Lernia Bemanning leads the top 10 with 397 MSEK operating profit over five years, while the biggest names by revenue (Randstad, Manpower, Adecco) were loss-making.

Electricity Prices SE1–SE4

Energy Day-ahead spot prices

Question: How far apart are electricity prices in northern and southern Sweden, when does the gap open, and when in the day is power most expensive? Daily day-ahead prices for all four bidding zones, January 2022 to November 2026 (7,160 zone-days, hourly and 15-minute data).
Findings: SE4 (Malmö) averaged 94.5 öre/kWh against 40.3 in SE1 (Luleå), more than twice as much. In November 2026 the SE4–SE1 gap reached 226 öre/kWh, the widest since August 2022. Southern prices peak around 08:00 and 18:00, and 7.5% of all price intervals in 2024 were negative.

Swedish Tourism & Guest Nights

Hospitality Tillväxtverket / SCB accommodation statistics

Question: Which regions depend most on one season, and on international guests? It started at Romme Alpin and Leksand: Dalarna's winter and summer peaks, compared across all 21 regions with monthly guest nights by visitor origin, 2015–2025.
Findings: 2025 was a record year with 70.7 million nights, 5% above 2019, after a 36% drop in 2020. July holds 20% of all nights nationally, but 41% in Kalmar and 35% on Gotland. Dalarna and Jämtland get 43% of their nights in January–March. The international share fell from 25% to 11% in 2020 and reached a record 26% in 2025.
